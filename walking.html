Footpaths from the door

Walking near Ullswater.

The Ullswater Way, Aira Force, Hallin Fell and Helvellyn — routes for every level, close to Westfield.

20 miles, waymarked

The Ullswater Way.

Opened in 2016, the Ullswater Way is a 20-mile waymarked circular route around the lake, created by a partnership between the Lake District National Park Authority, the National Trust, Eden District Council, Ullswater Steamers and the Ullswater Association. It's recognised by the Long Distance Walkers Association, and its daffodil waymark nods to Wordsworth's poem, inspired nearby.

You don't need to walk all 20 miles in one go — the route is designed to be broken into sections, using the steamer to shortcut between piers. Westfield's position in Watermillock, close to the western shore, puts local footpaths and stretches of the Way within easy reach of the cottage door.

Popular routes nearby

From gentle strolls to classic fells.

Easy

Aira Force & Gowbarrow

A woodland waterfall walk from the National Trust car park, with the option to continue up onto Gowbarrow Fell for wide views over Ullswater.

Easy

Hallin Fell

A short, steep climb from Martindale Church near Sandwick, with disproportionately good views over Howtown and the lake for the effort involved.

Moderate

Howtown to Glenridding

Often called one of the most beautiful lakeside walks in the Lake District, hugging the shore below Hallin Fell and Place Fell. Combine with a one-way steamer ticket.

Challenging

Helvellyn via Striding Edge

The classic route up England's third-highest mountain from Glenridding or Patterdale, with a famous knife-edge scramble — one of the busiest and best-loved days out in the Lakes.

Further afield

More fells around the valley.

Beyond Helvellyn, the Far Eastern Fells above Ullswater include Place Fell, St Sunday Crag and the Angletarn Pikes — all reachable from Glenridding or Patterdale for walkers wanting to get further from the lakeshore. For fully family-friendly options, the shorter loops around Aira Force, Hallin Fell and the Dalemain Loop near Pooley Bridge are the easiest introduction.

Map reference: the whole area is covered by Ordnance Survey Explorer OL5 (The English Lakes, North-eastern area).
